## The language of creation

In the 21st-century knowledge society, computer science is not just a subject; it is the language of our time. It's the tool that defines not only how we work, but how we think and solve humanity's biggest challenges. For a country, the ability to create technology —not just consume it— marks the difference between falling behind and its citizens' intellectual autonomy.

In Ecuador, although efforts have been made to integrate Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) into the education system, significant structural gaps persist that limit the development of deep digital competencies in students.

## Why computer science matters

Computer science shouldn't be understood merely as the use of technological tools, but as training in **computational thinking**, problem-solving, and systems development. According to Pérez Narváez (2017), computer science has achieved significant impact in both the workplace and society.

Several studies highlight that ICT boosts cognitive development and the capacity for innovation when properly integrated into educational processes. In this sense, computer science education is key to:

- **Empowering talent:** Preparing students to lead in a highly technological job market.
- **Fostering logic:** Developing analytical, logical, and abstraction skills.
- **Reducing dependency:** Driving local innovation and digital entrepreneurship.

## The reality we face

Despite its importance, implementation in Ecuador faces limitations. Recent research shows gaps in the effective integration of ICT, both in infrastructure and teacher training.

The pandemic exposed a concerning reality: both teachers and students show weaknesses in digital competencies, limiting how effectively technology can be leveraged in learning.

### Main problems identified:
- **Superficial approach:** Priority is given to basic tool usage (Word, Excel) instead of teaching programming or cybersecurity.
- **Digital divide:** Significant inequalities in access between urban and rural areas.
- **Outdated curricula:** They don't respond to the current demands of the tech job market.

## A constructive critique: beyond devices

The problem isn't the total absence of technology, but how it's implemented. **Equipping schools with computers doesn't guarantee quality education.** Without a deep pedagogical shift, these tools become mere extensions of the traditional teaching model.

Computer science should be recognized as a fundamental science, on par with mathematics or physics. Until that perception changes, we'll keep producing technology users instead of **architects of the future**.

## The path toward excellence

To transform this reality, we propose the following actions:

1. **Curricular restructuring:** Include programming and computational thinking from early grade levels.
2. **Continuous teacher training:** Training in advanced digital competencies and emerging technologies.
3. **Practical approach:** Project-Based Learning (PBL) and real systems development.
4. **Industry partnerships:** Alliances with tech companies for dual-training programs.

## Conclusion

Computer science education represents a strategic opportunity for Ecuador's development. Transforming this reality requires moving from training technology consumers to training creators and innovators. The country's future will depend on its ability to educate new generations in mastering computer science as a tool for **social transformation**.
